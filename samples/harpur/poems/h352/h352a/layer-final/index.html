Songs, Odes, and other Lyrics.

On Leaving x x x, after a residence there of several Months.

Though well nigh one whole year I’ve dwelt In this sequestered place, No ray of friendship have I felt 609 From any neighbor’s face: Ah! hearty human kindness Seems dying from the race.

We were not flowers of a like stem, Nor fruit of a like tree; For either I was too rare for them, Or they too rare for me: At all events, between us There was little sympathy.

They’d watched me gaze upon the Bay As forth alone I trod, And heard me when the shining day Lay broad upon the flood, Compare the stirless waters To a mighty sleeping God!

And thinking then, for such delight Such cause were wholly amiss They took me for some plotting wight Who feigned an idiot’s bliss, And chuckled to one another What manner of man is this?

They’d marked me climb the ranging height And from the points about Survey their homes, through visage bright So snugly looking out: Tracing some scheme of plunder They guessed I was, no doubt.

For on me then they’d fix their heed, There sitting in the sun, 610 As timely they my bent would read, Each to each passing one, Saying, with a look suspicious, What manner of man is yon?

Peace to their doubts. But what indeed Were Life, if all its way We found men such an oister-breed As these of Oister Bay?— I will not curse—nor bless them, Departing thus to-day.

And if, in their regard, one sense Within my heart I trace Too searching for indifference,— ‘Tis that so dull a race Should spoil by their known presence Even Nature of her grace!

Else were this Prospect noble—yea, ‘Tis noble in its own Old character,—the broad bright bay— The heights of pilëd stone, Standing, as from the Deluge They arose, all wildly lone!

My soul was social from my birth, And hence a tear doth start, That there should be one spot of earth Like this from which I part, So desert to my feelings— So empty to my heart!

Such must the cause be that I grieve— 611 No other can there be: There’s none—there’s nothing that I leave I wish again to see: Yet, as I go, I am yearning Like one in misery.
