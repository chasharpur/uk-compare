‘Tis life’s prime household wisdom not to scorn An erring Friend too fiercely. For if tried Severely in some weakness, all will turn To folly sometimes—till love’s cheek be dyed With blushes such as for the fallèn burn, For much even in the best hath Love to mourn. And knowing this, we should be sadly kind, When we reprove, not cruel, lest we churn Folly to madness!—Though in One we find Those gems of worth, high motives, feelings pure, Set in the fine gold of a constant mind? Yet even he shall sometimes prove insure! As in the clearest well a sediment lies, A sediment—which being disturbed, will rise
