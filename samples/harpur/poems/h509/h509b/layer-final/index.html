IX

As when a stubborn ass, caught trespassing, Will cease not from devouring the tall corn Though many a sturdy stick on his gaunt sides Already hath been smashed, and still the boys Keep spending all their feeble strength, in still Repeated thwacks upon his sounding hide; Nor can they; till the brute is cramed with food, Expel him, and with difficulty then: So the great-hearted Trojans & with their far Derived auxilliaries, still following, throng The mighty Ajax,—sorely taxing all His stubborn valor, and from every side Stricking his broad shield with the flying spears While often wheeling, in the consious pride Impetuous vigor, now he holds in check The wedging fronts of the horse-taming Trojans; Then, turning warily, again retreats
