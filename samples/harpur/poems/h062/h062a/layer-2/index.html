The House of Riot.

It is the House of Riot—the chosen place Of much vain speech and simulated mirth, And fleshly feasting. From its dissolute door They have spurned Simplicity, and red-lipt Health, With native Cheerfulness, and Strength, and long- Forecasting Prudence, and domestic Peace, And Piety who loves to dwell with these; And as their household Deities instead, Have welcomed there Extravagance disguised As Bounty, joined with riotess Excess, And false Enjoyment, and false knowledge dressed In the stol’n airs and habit of a sage; And Atheism mantled in the stole Of a denuded Prophet.

Now, even now While Thought’s keen spirit lightens through my brain, Intent to benefit the Young, to thee, O Wisdom, do I cry, and thee invoke175 To aid the voice that I would pour abroad In a melodious though a warning song! So pure the inspiring motive, that ‘twere well If thou shouldst swell its monitory tones With heaven’s own thunder.

Hear, then, ye who would Be early wise! my chosen words shall be Meet robes for Wisdom in her holiest plight. And know ye not that even the reprobate, Though Godless, must admit, that he who scorns Instruction in the morning of his life (of all forsaken) Shall, a grey prodigal, sit down with Shame And ineffectual Remorse, to weep Its setting sun away.

And, verily, That froward-hearted Youth who shall esteem The Preacher’s words but as the profitless stones Roughening the forehead of some sterile hill, With aching limbs swoll’n into hideous shape, Haggard and brown and stark, he at the mill176 Of sordid toil, even to the dregs of life, Shall grind for others more than for himself.

O we should honor Wisdom! since through her The great Immutable of Heaven devised The boundless world. Yea, she it was who first Elanced the sun on his imperial course, Day’s monarch, vested with celestial fire; Her hand it was that fashioned the round moon And all the golden stars. Or couldst thou soar Up to the throne of God, even there amid Unspeakable splendors she were found of thee; Or to the unfathomable gulfs of Hell Down couldst thou plunge, behold, there, even there Wisdom would meet thee, though obscured, as when The day in its meridian strength is seen Darkened with storms.

Return we to the House Of Riot, the chosen set place of much vain speech, And fleshly feasting, and pretensive mirth. 177 There congregate the frivolous of heart, As garrulous parrots gather to the snares Set on the borders of the ripening maize. There Phrenzy in the name of earnest Joy, With wild embarrassed brow, o’erflows the cups With blazing wine, while Passion in the plight Of Love inflames his horrid fires withal. There too Intemperance takes his constant round As seneshal, high heaping every board With palatable death, while round them drew The gluttonous, like swine about their troughs Sluic’d with slab garbage.

That fatal House! Shun it all ye in thought yet young, in heart Unguarded, and all ye of of facile wills; Lest, like its host, ye with like years should cast To sow the world for an abundant yield Of pleasure,—and mistaken, only reap An overwhelming load of debt, instead,— Tf debt, distaste and vanity. and vexation.

Enter once there, and marvel at the change That in thy moral being shall be wrought. Boisterous thy speech shall soon become, but ah! Thy reason silent as the tongue of death; Thine evil passions as the surging flames Of some obstreperous forest fire, but all Thy virtues, like the wintry crust that numbs The living brook till it forgets to flow.

Then ‘neath the growing influence of the fierce And fatal spirit of that dissolute House, So recklessly familiar shalt thou grow With splendid Ruin, as to kneel and kiss Her jewell’d hand, and say unto her—Thou Shalt be my Spouse; and for thy sake, behold Here stand I—ready to impawn at once The peals of truth—the jewels of my peace— The rubies of repute—yea all wherewith I once adorned my soul, for lures and gauds And glistering baubles, wherewithal to deck My body, dedicate henceforth to thee.

Into her inmost secret chambers then179 In desperate fondness shalt thou follow her; While lovely Phantoms beckon thee along: Lovely—but fleeting as the shapes that crest An evening cloud: lovely—but dangerous As that mysterious light which glides about Some midnight marsh, misleading him who sees. And songs mellifluous as those which he, The enthusiast, when to reason dying, hears In mountain glens, or wooded vales, or near Some lone lake crimsoned by the setting sun, Shall hail thy presence, cloying all the air With sweetness,—and awakening raptures sweet And soul-subduing, but delusive all As are the hopes of unrequited love: Sweet—sweet—but cruel as untimely death!

Then with the histories of royal lust In her right hand displayed, and in her left A chronicle wherein the eminent damned Are registered as wise, thy yielded heart180 She shall baptize in the lewd mysteries Of fashionable folly; and at last Instruct thee, with a devil’s zeal, how best May be down smitten the arising thought Prophetic, that would soar as with a stork’s Unwearying wings, from Earth away,—away Through the Eternal and the Infinite, To rest at length in Heaven.

—Who is this, That in yon chariot comes flaming on, In costliest raiment clad, and tricked with gauds Of gold and glittering jewels?

Who knoweth not The sun of Fashion and the revellers’ star?

I know not him you mean. But who is this With glittering ornaments bedizzened so, That he himself is lost in their display?

Even he—that charioteer, is Fashion’s sun, 181 And the presiding star of Revelry. Is there a House for deep carousals famed, He is dictator there; and where the vain Of heart are, there is he.

Wouldst thou he should Promote the blest and blessing cause of Charity? The shoulder-srugging ribald shall reply He loves not a cold mistress! Wouldst thou he Should aid prophetic Genius, or assist Poor Learning, laboring in the mines of Truth To enrich a thankless world? The miscreant’s means Are all forestalled! What if his Country’s need Besought her sons to gather like a storm And sweep Invasion from her sacred coasts, Or thunder signal condemnation down On Kingcraft, and on all internal wrong? Her need might move him not: for he is one Whose energies long since were drenched away In midnight orgies by the flowing bowl.

182

Go seek of him in thine extremity Condolence and advice:—No search so vain! The veriest frensy shall he froth thee forth. With the high polish of his boasted wit Go burnish thou thy studious front! Alas! ‘Tis but a tinselled tissue of the arts That celebrate the stews. His usefulness Hath passed already with his yesterdays; His understanding, with like lumber, rots Already in Oblivion’s dismal den.

Then in the House of Riot seek ye not For pleasure, nor for knowledge from its vain And gaudy Paracites, ye young ones, lest A taint of madness for the first ye catch, And for the other wed a lasting folly. Rather seek Wisdom in the house of woe; Or meditate her amid ruined tombs Charged with the dust of ages,—dust that once Was the warm human beauty of the world.

183

Thus far the Preacher warneth: and although His lot be lowly and his years but few, Despise ye not his words; for he,—even he Hath labored with unflinching heart the field Where grey Experience sows her golden grain; Reapt there for knowledge with a strenuous care, And threshed and ground for excellence of speech! Yea, Thought hath loved him from his earliest years, And when a child, consorted with his mates In the old wood, or by the stream that past Its reedy marge went simmering, he hath looked With eye prophetic of the coming storm Upon the unguarded flowers, his pregnant words Have yeilded fruit even to aged men Who chanced to hear them as they passed along.
