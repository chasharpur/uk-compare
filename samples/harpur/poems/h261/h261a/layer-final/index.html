AUSTRALIAN LYRICS.—No. 1.

THE BOWER.

Idalia, in thy favorite shade A pleasant bower I’ve made, That thou, what time the sun is high, Mayst shroud thee from the casual eye, And by the bright brook gushing by O’ersprinkled be: Emblem of purity and thee.

Of two mimosas, linking o’er, I formed the rural door; A larger growth, half circling round, I bent into a dome, and bound, And with their trunks then interwound Each lesser tree: E’en as my arms entwine with thee.

Around the whole the bush woodbine I’ve taught in knots to twine; And the wild fig-tree’s ample bough Have led the myrtles so to woo, That mingled on the breeze they flow, Graceful to see: That clasping this as I do thee.

And, as the blooms of all unfold, An emerald dome, with gold Inlaid, and richly studded through With gems of many a mingled hue, It seemeth; ruddy, purple, blue— Such gems as be Rare in their preciousness, like thee.

And soon shall be its levelled floor With flower plants paven o’er; Those lovely beings that dispense A sweetness round, which, to the sense, Is like a voiceless eloquence! And they shall be My fellow subjects unto thee. Come and see.

Charles Harpur.
