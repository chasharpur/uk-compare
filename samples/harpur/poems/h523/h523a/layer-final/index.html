Song

O, there’s nothing worth loving that money can buy There’s nothing worth sighing for ever was sold! An outside of splendor may dazzle the eye— But a warm honest heart still is Nature’s true gold.

In my head, when I started in life, was this song— Or rather its prnciple temper, or plan: So I vowed ne’er to value—(and could I be wrong?) Any buyable, sellable daughter of Man.

First Gussy inspired me—fired me—she Whom I dreamt of when dreaming of roses of gold; I thought I loved her and she thought she loved me; But ah! being sellable, Gussy was sold.

Her falshood was sudden—but lucky no less! And wholesome the tear was it brought to my eye: For it nourished the thought that none else might possess A girl worth my loving that money could buy.

Next Peggy’s fine eye lit a flame in my breast; I relished her her music, and, tasting her lip, Thought of marriage, but bringing her heart to the test And finding it buyable—gave her the slip.

A rich handsome Widow succeeded—and Oh, How she laboured to liquor me into the ring! But her thinking me buyable, gave me to know That herself was at best but a sellable thing.

So I turned from her banquets; still true to my creed, And resolving thenceforth but to court on the sly: Let the moralists twit me, I said, but indeed, There’s none worth my wedding that money can buy.

Well; onward I went in the paths of unrest, And if life’s end were passion—its all was my own, But Oh, there was ever an ake in my breast For some one to wed me for love’s sake alone.

Thus void was my heart, when with Mary I met, The Girl who could wed me for Love’s sake alone: I was friendless and poor—but how valleantly yet, For that heart, having won it, she gave me her own!

Her own—though the truest and fondest in love That ever grew big with a passionate sigh! Hence how happily now can I sing as I rove— Oh, there’s nothing worth loving that money can buy.
