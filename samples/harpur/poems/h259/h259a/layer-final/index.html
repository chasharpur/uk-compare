ORIGINAL POETRY.

A SONG FOR THE SPRING TIME.

The mimosas are blooming, For summer is coming, I felt her warm breath in the forest to-day: Where the river is streaming, And Nature lies dreaming Of new love and beauty, come, dearest, away.

His gentle mate wooing, The wood-pigeon’s cooing In the oaks that o’ershadow the path we will take: Like music out flowing, Come forth, that all glowing And beautiful things may please more for your sake.

We will wander, joy drinking, Until the sun, sinking, Shall give the deep west with his glory to blaze; When homeward returning, With poesy burning, I’ll mint from those splendours a song in your praise.

Charles Harpur.

Jerry’s Plains, 10th September, 1843.
